**What it means**

- Evaluates situations primarily on facts and outcomes, with less attention to the emotional undercurrent in the room
- Notices emotional cues mainly when they're pointed out directly or the setting is calm, not automatically
- Tends to treat "how people feel about it" as separate from "what's the right call"
- Can be personally difficult to read, even when not intending to be

**Strengths**

- Stays task-focused and unaffected by emotional noise in the room
- Makes decisions without being swayed by how people will feel about them
- Steady under conditions that would rattle a more emotionally reactive leader
- Doesn't personalize conflict or take feedback as an attack

**Focus on**

- Building a habit of checking in on how a decision landed, not just whether it was correct
- Naming feelings explicitly when they come up, since they often won't be inferred without being said
- Asking directly how someone is doing, especially before or after a high-stress period, rather than assuming things are fine because nothing was said
- Watching for trust eroding quietly — it often shows up as people going around a leader rather than to them
