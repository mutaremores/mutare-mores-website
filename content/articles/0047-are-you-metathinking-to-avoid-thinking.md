* Metathinking is thinking about your thinking. Example: Me - What's 4 x 5? You - 20 of course. (Also you, in your head) - How did I know that so quick? Did I do a calculation that fast or was it just memorized?
* Of course thinking is just thinking, you are working through something internally and not paying much thought to the actual thoughts in your head, just the external problem at hand.
* The problem is that many hyper-self aware people will get into this habit of metathinking when the thinking gets hard. I do it myself quite often.
* Example: Why did I think that just now? What assumptions did I make to get to that conclusion? Was that a bias?
* I've noticed that occasionally, people will do this to avoid actually thinking about the problem at hand. It's fascinating, you are thinking so hard that your brain would rather think about its own thoughts.
* It's not that it's bad to meta think, it's actually a very good thing, and more people should do it because that's how you build self awareness, clarity, and challenge your own thoughts to avoid bias.
* The problem is that we can do this to avoid thinking about the problem. Now not only are you using up more of your brain's energy, but it's not even directed at what it needs to be!
* If you find yourself in this predicament often, you can either a.) snap yourself out of it, or b.) take a break.
* I usually try to snap out of it a couple times, and if I quickly go back to thinking about my thinking, I will then just take a short 5-10 minutes break and go for a walk or eat something.
