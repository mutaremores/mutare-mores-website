**What it means**

- Adjusts quickly when plans, information, or circumstances change, without needing much recovery time
- Functions well with incomplete information rather than waiting for full certainty before acting
- Treats disruption as a normal condition to work within, not an emergency
- Comfortable holding a loose plan that's expected to change, rather than a fixed one

**Strengths**

- Thrives in flux — setbacks barely slow them down
- Functions well under ambiguity and incomplete information
- Steadies the team during disruption instead of adding to it
- Recovers quickly from a plan not working out, without losing momentum

**Focus on**

- Giving a new direction time to actually work before moving to the next one — not every plan needs a pivot
- Communicating changes clearly enough that the rest of the team can track what's current, since what's obvious to a highly adaptable leader isn't always obvious to others
- Watching for a pattern of the newest idea always looking better than the one already in motion
- Building in occasional stability on purpose, even when it isn't strictly necessary, so the team has something steady to hold onto
