* Developed in Japan in the 1980s
* Two sets of principles: I's and H's

+ Interactive - Humans participate in creating and decision-making, not just consuming outputs from models/tools
+ Intelligent - Knowledge systems should learn, adapt, and incorporate expertise instead of just retrieve data
+ Interdisciplinary - Multiple areas of expertise are used, because no single discipline can accurately represent complex reality
+ Honesty - Models should faithfully represent uncertainty and limitations
+ Humanity - Systems should support human judgment instead of replacing it
+ Harmony - Collaboration among stakeholders is considered part of the solution itself

* In other words, in order to make the best choices and really do what is best for the future, we need to incorporate more opinions, knowledge, and input instead of relying solely on one tool, person, or model to give us an answer.
* This is traditionally in relation to systems design/systems thinking, where researchers will analyze complex environments to try and understand it better, but these tend to stray from reality due to the complex nature of the world we live in, put simply, we can't predict the future, so we shouldn't expect some mathematical model to do it either.
