**What it means**

- Prefers stability and clear rules — a well-defined process feels safer and more reliable than an open-ended one
- Change, especially unplanned change, is genuinely disruptive rather than a minor adjustment
- Needs more time and information than a highly adaptable leader before feeling ready to shift direction
- Builds real depth by staying with an approach long enough to master it, rather than moving on quickly

**Strengths**

- Brings real consistency and deep expertise within a stable lane
- Predictable — people know what they're going to get
- Strong when the environment doesn't require constant change
- Sees a plan through rather than abandoning it at the first sign of difficulty

**Focus on**

- Practicing making a decision with incomplete information on lower-stakes situations, to build comfort before it's forced by a real crisis
- Naming the discomfort of a needed change directly, rather than delaying it while waiting to feel more ready
- Building a small "what if this changes" contingency into plans upfront, so a shift doesn't require starting over
- Noticing the cost of staying the course too long — sometimes the harder call is the more adaptable one
