**What it means**

- Relies on position, facts, or established relationships to move people, rather than persuasion as a practiced skill
- Effective one-on-one or in small, familiar groups, less so scaling that to a room of strangers
- States a case plainly and lets it stand on its own merits rather than building a deliberate pitch
- Doesn't naturally reach for storytelling or framing to make an argument land

**Strengths**

- Straightforward — doesn't rely on manipulation or persuasion tactics to get buy-in
- Effective in close, familiar relationships where trust is already established
- What they say is what they mean; no hidden agenda in how they influence
- Earns durable trust precisely because they aren't seen as "working" people

**Focus on**

- Practicing framing a case for a specific audience rather than presenting it the same way regardless of who's listening
- Speaking up earlier and more often in group settings, where quieter voices are easy to lose
- Building relationships beyond the immediate circle, since influence without an existing relationship is a learnable skill, not a fixed trait
- Recognizing that being right isn't the same as being convincing — a good idea still needs a case made for it
