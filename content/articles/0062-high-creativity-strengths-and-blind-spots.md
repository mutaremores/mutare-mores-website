**What it means**

- Creativity is a default mode, not something switched on only when asked — new angles show up unprompted, in routine work as much as big projects
- Constraints (tight budget, short deadline, limited people) tend to sharpen thinking rather than shut it down
- Comfortable holding multiple possible approaches at once instead of collapsing quickly to "the" answer
- Genuinely energized by ambiguous or undefined problems, where the first step isn't obvious

**Strengths**

- Regularly reframes problems and offers alternative approaches instead of defaulting to the first idea
- Turns constraints into fuel — creative output often increases, not decreases, under real limits
- A strong brainstorming partner who unblocks teams stuck on a single approach
- Spots non-obvious connections between unrelated ideas, people, or projects

**Focus on**

- Setting a hard stop for idea generation, so exploration doesn't quietly replace a decision that needs to get made
- Noticing when interest fades once a project moves from "figuring it out" to repetitive execution — and building in accountability for the finish, not just the start
- Checking the team's actual pace before introducing another new idea; more options aren't always useful mid-execution
- Pairing with someone execution-minded (or deliberately building execution habits) so good ideas don't stall before they ship
