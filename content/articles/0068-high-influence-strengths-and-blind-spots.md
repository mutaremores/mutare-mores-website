**What it means**

- Moves people to act, agree, or change their mind regardless of formal title or authority
- Adjusts the pitch depending on the audience — what lands with one group won't necessarily land with another, and that's accounted for
- Comfortable building support before a decision is made official, not just announcing it afterward
- Reads what actually matters to a given person or group and leads with that, rather than a one-size-fits-all argument

**Strengths**

- Earns buy-in across audiences without needing to lean on formal authority
- Mobilizes people effectively, including cross-functionally
- Builds coalitions and gets people moving in the same direction
- Turns a good idea into something people will actually champion, not just tolerate

**Focus on**

- Checking that an idea has been fully vetted before selling it hard — persuasiveness can outrun the underlying substance if it isn't
- Making room for quieter voices in the room rather than letting natural presence dominate the conversation
- Being transparent about the ask, rather than relying on charisma to smooth over a case that hasn't been made explicit
- Noticing when "getting buy-in" starts to matter more than whether the decision itself is the right one
