* Techniques
* Mind maps

+ These are great for connecting things you've learned, the most common way is to start with a central theme, question, topic, or inspiration and branch out from there. Use in conjunction with other techniques to really maximize how effective your mind maps are.

* Free write/ brain dump

+ Just let it out! It doesn't matter if it's by writing, speaking, drawing, whatever. Just get something out and don't think too hard about it.

* SCAMPER

+ For something that currently exists, think about ways you could: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse

* Six thinking hats

+ Looking through different lenses (or 'putting on different hats') will help you see things from a different perspective.

* Randomization

+ Try and be as random as possible to get your brain thinking creatively. Put words on slips of paper and mix them up. Do the "One word at a time" game in a group.

* The 5 Whys

+ When confronted with a problem that you can't seem to find the answer to, ask "Why?" five times, and the real answer will likely be there.

* Reverse brainstorming

+ Name the problem, now, instead of trying to brainstorm solutions, try and think of all the ways you could make it worse

* Nominal group technique

+ Everyone in the group writes down their ideas, thoughts, concerns, etc. Gather the answers, compile into a master list, discuss, and privately rate each 1-5. Compile the votes to find the winning option(s).
