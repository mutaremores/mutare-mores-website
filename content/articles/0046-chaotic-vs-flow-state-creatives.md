* Flow state creatives tend to do their best work during long blocks of time where they can fully focus on their work, without distractions or interruptions
* Chaotic creatives tend to do their best work in the midst of chaos, pressure, and in general just having a constantly changing environment or mental state
* One isn't better or worse than the other, but knowing which you are, and restricting yourself to avoid the downsides of each will help you stay creative and productive in the long run
* Flow state creatives:

+ Tend to lead more peaceful, stress-free lives
+ Have a dedicated time and place they can work on their tasks in order to avoid distractions
+ They are consistent, focusing on steady work instead of rushing to do everything
+ They can do lots of work in a short time period thanks to the flow state, and it usually passes by quickly for them
+ Can leave behind other important tasks like eating, socializing, or getting other things done that might not be as enjoyable
+ They may feel less creative than they would like

* If this is you (flow state):

+ Keep a dedicated time to get yourself into the flow state on a regular schedule, this way you can eventually make it a part of your dedicated routine and block it off
+ If inspiration hits, don't be afraid to stop what you're doing and work on something else
+ Make an effort to go out of your routine every-so-often, find inspiration from unfamiliar things
+ Set a no-exceptions time limit. The flow state is addicting, it feels amazing to not have anything else going through your mind and have time fly by, but this causes other issues that, without a strict time limit for you to stop your work, can snowball into serious issues down the road

* Chaotic creatives:

+ Tend to have a never-ending to-do list and are always busy
+ Have lists of projects that they are working on, 90% only halfway started
+ Wait for moments of inspiration to hit within their crazy schedule
+ Cluttered environments but know where everything is

* If this is you (chaotic):

+ Be weary of burnout, if you find yourself missing events and commitments or tasks you need to do, start dialing back your schedule and leave some free time for yourself to just think and be
+ Establish some form of routine, whether it be 5-minute walks outside in the morning, or working on only your most important project for the first 2 hours of the day
+ Take time to reflect on your day, what you've done, what you need to do tomorrow, and notice how you feel about it all
+ If you work with others, and they complain or can't find things from your area, try and reorganize to help them so they can be at their best too
