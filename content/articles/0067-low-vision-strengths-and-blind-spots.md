**What it means**

- Oriented toward what's happening right now — today's problems, this week's priorities — more than a multi-year direction
- Can state a goal or direction when asked, but it doesn't come up unprompted in day-to-day decisions
- More comfortable executing within a direction someone else has set than generating that direction
- Judges progress by what got done, not by how it connects to a longer-term story

**Strengths**

- Highly responsive to what's actually happening day-to-day
- Doesn't get distracted by abstract future-state thinking when there's real work in front of them
- Comfortable letting others supply the "why" while focusing on the "what"
- Keeps plans grounded in present reality instead of over-indexing on a future that may not play out as imagined

**Focus on**

- Naming a direction out loud, even a simple one, so the team has something to orient around beyond this week's task list
- Noticing when the team seems to be drifting or losing motivation — that's often a signal that a "why" is missing, not just a "what"
- Revisiting stated goals regularly rather than only when directly asked, so they actually stick
- Partnering with someone vision-oriented, or deliberately blocking time to think beyond the immediate horizon
