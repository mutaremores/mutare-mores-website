**What it means**

- Reaches for known, proven approaches first, and generally sees that as the responsible choice rather than a limitation
- Generates a new idea when the situation calls for one, but it isn't a running habit
- More comfortable executing a clear plan than sitting in an undefined, ambiguous problem
- Trusts what's already been tested over what's merely interesting

**Strengths**

- Reliable, low-risk execution — sticks with approaches that are known to work
- Filters out impractical ideas quickly; stays grounded in what's realistic
- Predictable to work with, and doesn't introduce unnecessary change
- Keeps a team focused rather than chasing every new idea that comes up

**Focus on**

- Building a habit of asking "is there a different way to do this" before defaulting to the familiar approach, even when the familiar one seems to be working
- Creating space for the team to float alternative ideas without it feeling like second-guessing the plan
- Noticing early when a standard playbook stops fitting a situation, rather than after it's clearly failed
- Borrowing creative thinking deliberately — a second opinion, a structured brainstorm, someone whose default mode is generating options
